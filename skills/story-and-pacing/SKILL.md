# Story and pacing

## Hook (first 10 seconds)
- Open on the most striking image and a bold claim or question. Throne rooms, burning suns and lone figures in the snow all work.
  - Example: "He rules a kingdom of ice and gold. But how did a boy from nowhere end up on this throne?"
- Start with the payoff, then jump back: "To answer that, we have to go back…"
- No intros, channel names or "in this video".

## Narration
- Use short sentences, one idea per line, spoken the way a storyteller talks.
- Each scene is 1–3 sentences, about 4–9 seconds. A new scene is a new place or idea, so cut often.
- Keep a curiosity gap open: end beats on a turn ("But the baron hated them.", "And that changed everything.").
- Use concrete details (names, places, numbers) over vague statements.

## Visual rhythm
- Alternate story scenes that have characters with motion-graphic beats. Use `earth` for a place, `stat` for a number, `timeline` for years, `portraits` for "people who…", and `sun`, `space` or `network` for big ideas.
- Never place the same scene type more than twice in a row. Vary `time: night`, `snow`, palettes and camera `push`.
- Give a character a `tag=` the first time they appear, so the name label pops in on their close-up.
- Use `react` shots when someone is threatened or surprised. Set `mood=worried` on listeners and `mood=angry` on villains.
- The default cut is 1.8 seconds. Use `cut: 1.3` for action and `cut: 2.5` for calm, sad moments.

## Character design
- Characters are chunky and big-headed. Make each one readable at a glance with one strong colour and one headwear or hair silhouette.
- Keep a character identical across scenes by copying their exact `cast:` entry.
- Kids use `kid`. Elders use `old` with a grey `beard`. Soldiers use `hat=helmet, outfit=tunic, prop=sword`. Royals use `hat=crown, outfit=robe`.
- Make every character original. For a real story about an existing fictional character, design an original stand-in and give it a new name.

## Length
- **Short or YouTube Short:** 6–8 scenes, 30–45 seconds.
- **Standard:** 10–16 scenes, 1–2 minutes.
- **Long:** split into chapters, each opened with a `label:` ribbon.
